Risk Awareness Week 2026

Five questions for any risk model

The next time someone shows you a number, a loss estimate, a frequency, a VaR, a risk score, ask these five questions before you act on it.

Download PDF
1

Where does the data come from, and when was it last updated?

Every source has its own biases. Insurance claims data is censored. Breach databases depend on reporting thresholds. Vendor telemetry reflects their customer base, not yours. If you cannot trace each material input to a documented source, you do not know what the model is actually measuring.

2

Which inputs matter most, and how well grounded are they?

Most model outputs are driven by a handful of parameters. If the top driver is an expert estimate that has never been tested against data, that is where your model risk is concentrated. Sensitivity analysis tells you where to focus your validation effort.

3

What is the uncertainty band, and has anyone reported it?

When the plausible range spans two orders of magnitude, presenting a point estimate without qualification creates a false sense of certainty. The primary output of model validation should be an uncertainty band, never a single point estimate.

4

What does the model leave out, and does it matter for this decision?

Every model simplifies reality. Common omissions include dependence between events, accumulation risk, and second-order effects. Each omission biases the output in a direction, optimistic or pessimistic. Know which direction, and whether it changes the decision.

5

Is this model designed for the decision you are making?

A model sizing a $200 million insurance program needs full uncertainty quantification. A model for internal prioritization needs documented assumptions and periodic self-assessment. A model unreliable for absolute loss prediction may still be reliable for comparing alternatives: the ranking can be right when the numbers are not. Know whether the model is being used for the decision it was designed to support.

These five questions are drawn from a framework of ten model risk checks presented in the RAW 2026 talk “Can you trust that number? Ten checks on the assumptions and data behind any risk model.”

The full framework, including validation catalogs, tail estimation guidance, and a structured vendor assessment checklist, is available in the ERQI Model Risk paper.
